# Course Syllabus — Power BI for Forensic Accounting, Data Analytics, ML & AI

**Provider:** Maxhub Pvt Ltd
**Learner profile:** accounting / auditing background, new to Power BI, targeting a services practice
**Total effort:** ~90–110 hours (modules, labs, capstones)
**Prerequisites:** comfortable with debits and credits, a trial balance and a P&L. No coding needed.

---

## 1. Learning outcomes

By the end of this course you can, unaided:

1. Connect Power BI to messy accounting data and turn it into a clean, documented star schema.
2. Write DAX measures that survive an accountant's review: YTD, closing balances, variance, ratios.
3. Produce a live financial reporting pack (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, KPI page) from raw ledger data.
4. Design dashboards for executives, auditors and — where needed — legal proceedings.
5. Run a full forensic analytics programme: Benford, duplicate testing, ghost vendors, threshold
   testing, journal-entry risk scoring, segregation-of-duties analysis.
6. Investigate a suspected fraud end-to-end and produce defensible working papers and a report.
7. Build machine-learning and AI-assisted analyses inside Power BI (AutoML, Key Influencers,
   anomaly detection, forecasting, Python/R visuals, Copilot).
8. Package, price and deliver all of the above as Maxhub service lines with templates, standards
   and quality control.

## 5. What "done" looks like

You have finished this course when you can hand a client the following, without help:

- A `.pbix` file that refreshes from their ledger extract without manual intervention.
- A one-page methodology note describing what the model does and what its limits are.
- A findings or insights pack with numbers that reconcile to their trial balance.
- A price, a scope and an engagement letter for the next phase of work.

That is a **Maxhub engagement deliverable**, and it is what the capstones simulate.

## 6. Study method that actually works

1. **Read the module once, quickly.** Do not try to memorise DAX on the first pass.
2. **Do the lab with the module open.** Type the code yourself; do not copy-paste until it works.
3. **Break it.** Change a filter, remove a relationship, delete a measure's DIVIDE. Watching it fail
   teaches context faster than watching it succeed.
4. **Reconcile.** Every model must tie to a control total. The labs tell you which total.
5. **Write the methodology note.** If you cannot explain the calculation in plain English to a
   partner, you do not own it yet.
6. **Record your work.** Keep a running "evidence and method" log per capstone. This becomes your
   portfolio and your quality-control evidence.
