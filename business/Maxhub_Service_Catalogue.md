## 1. Forensic accounting

### 1.1 Forensic Investigation
**Client problem:** suspected misappropriation, whistle-blower allegation, unexplained margin erosion,
a dispute with a supplier or an ex-employee.

**What we do:** run a 12-test analytics programme across 24+ months of ledger data (duplicates, ghost
vendors, threshold and split-payment testing, journal-entry testing, segregation-of-duties analysis,
Benford), investigate the exceptions to source documents, quantify the loss, and report.

**Deliverables:** findings report · evidence register · quantified loss schedule · remediation plan ·
board presentation · expert-ready working papers.

**Indicative fee:** $8,000 – $45,000 depending on data volume and period.
**Duration:** 3 – 8 weeks.

### 1.2 Procurement Risk Scan (entry product)
**Client problem:** "we do not know if our payments are clean."
**What we do:** one year of spend data, 12 automated tests, exception register, a findings summary with
the top 10 items for follow-up.
**Deliverables:** exception register (Excel) · 12-test summary · 90-minute debrief.
**Indicative fee:** $2,500 – $6,000. **Duration:** 5 – 10 working days.
**Purpose:** this is the door-opener. Roughly one in three scans leads to a larger engagement.

### 1.3 Fraud Risk Assessment
**Client problem:** boards need evidence of fraud risk management, not a policy document.
**What we do:** interview key staff, walk the processes, test 100% of the transaction population for
control exceptions, score inherent and residual risk per cycle, design the controls that would have
prevented each exception found.
**Deliverables:** fraud risk register (scored) · control gap analysis · 12-month monitoring plan.
**Indicative fee:** $6,000 – $18,000.

### 1.4 Continuous Monitoring retainer
**Deliverables:** monthly exception report from the client's own data, reviewed by a Maxhub analyst;
quarterly control review.
**Indicative fee:** $900 – $2,500 per month.

## 6. How we price (rules of thumb)

1. **Never price the tool; price the outcome.** "Eight days of month-end down to three" is a number the
   client can value. "A dashboard" is not.
2. **Estimate hours, then add 30%** for data quality and the client's own variations in format.
3. **Fixed fee for defined scope; time and materials for discovery.** Investigations are always
   time-based with a weekly review of the burn.
4. **Retainers are the business plan.** A build is a project; a retainer is an annuity. Every build
   proposal should carry a proposed retainer line.
5. **Charge for the whole engagement:** data request, kick-off, fieldwork, review, report, handover,
   and the QC review. Many firms forget the QC review — do not, it is what protects you.
6. **Publish your day rates** in proposals. Vague pricing costs more in negotiation time than it earns.

## 7. Qualification checklist (do not propose before you can answer these)

- [ ] Who is the decision maker and what do they want to be true?
- [ ] What data exists, in what format, for what period, and who extracts it?
- [ ] Is there a deadline that makes this urgent (audit, bank, board, regulator)?
- [ ] What is the budget, and who signs?
- [ ] Do we have the skills, the capacity and no independence conflict?
- [ ] What is the smallest engagement that proves value and opens the next one?
